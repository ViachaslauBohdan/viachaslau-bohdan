---
title: What a CTO should expect from me
description: The working agreement I use when I join a company as a senior developer — scope, access, and what “done” means.
date: 2026-08-12
---

You should know who is writing the code, what they will not build, and when you will see it running.

When a company brings me in, the first week is about the boundary of the work. Which user journey matters. What already exists. What is blocked. What can wait until after launch. I write that down so we are not discovering the real project in month two.

A few things I treat as normal, not as extras:

- You get a direct line. Questions do not go through an account manager.
- Progress is visible every week: a demo, a deployed slice, or a written note on what changed and what is stuck.
- Architecture choices come with the trade-off. If I recommend waiting on a rewrite, I say what breaks if we wait and what breaks if we do not.
- The handover is part of the job. Your team should be able to run, change, and deploy the work after I step back.

I am a poor fit if you need a large team yesterday, a fixed bid on an undefined product, or someone to silently absorb a roadmap that has not been cut. I am a good fit when a CTO needs one senior person to own a product slice and tell the truth about it.
